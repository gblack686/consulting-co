# My 2025 Job Search by the Numbers

After 22 months of job searching, I built an AI-powered system to analyze my Gmail and Calendar data. Here's what I found.

---

## The Funnel

```
272 Applications
    ↓
235 Unique Companies
    ↓
63 Interview Events (23%)
    ↓
16 Companies with Interviews (6%)
    ↓
2 Offers (0.7%)
```

**Translation:** For every 136 applications, I received 1 offer.

## Key Insights

### What the Data Revealed:

1. **Response Rate:** Only 6% of applications led to an interview
2. **Interview-to-Offer:** 12.5% of companies I interviewed with made an offer
3. **The "Ghost Zone":** 70% of applications received no response at all
4. **Speed Matters:** Both offers came from processes under 40 days
5. **Take-Homes:** Completed 3 take-home assignments, 0 converted to offers

### The Hidden Costs:

- 63 hours in interviews (assuming 1hr avg)
- 3 take-home projects (8-15 hrs each)
- 272 applications (avg 20 min each = 90+ hours)

**Total estimated time invested:** 175+ hours

---

## What I Learned

1. **Volume is necessary but not sufficient.** 272 applications for 2 offers means the market is brutal.

2. **Interview count doesn't predict offers.** My highest interview count (9 rounds) hasn't resulted in an offer. Both actual offers came from 3-4 round processes.

3. **LinkedIn dominates but doesn't differentiate.** 39% of my applications were through LinkedIn, but neither offer came from a LinkedIn Easy Apply.

4. **The 30-day rule.** If a company hasn't made a decision in 30 days, they probably won't. Both offers came within 27-39 days.

5. **Ghosting is the norm.** Expecting closure is setting yourself up for disappointment. Move on mentally after 2 weeks of silence.

## How I Built This

I used Claude Code to build a Gmail analysis system that:
- Connected to Gmail API via OAuth
- Classified 272 job-related emails using AI
- Synced with Google Calendar for actual interview counts
- Tracked correspondence, deliverables, and timelines
- Stored everything in a Supabase database

The whole system was built in a single afternoon.
