## Answers

### A1
**C) Template Your Engineering**

### A2
**B) A prompt that builds a prompt**
Meta-prompts build other prompts based on templates.

### A3
**B) A plan, specification, or PRD**
Plans are prompts scaled up for high impact.

### A4
**B) A prompt that accepts another prompt as input**
HOPs take other prompts as input - plans passed into plans.

### A5
**C) The reasoning model**
"Think hard" activates extended reasoning capabilities.

### A6
**B) The specs directory**
All generated plans are written to the specs directory.

### A7
**B) 80% of agentic impact with 20% complexity**
The Pareto principle applied to agentic engineering fundamentals.

### A8
**B) Entire classes of problems**
Templates solve problem classes, not just individual problems.

### A9
**B) It's time consuming and scales with problem difficulty**
Planning time increases with problem difficulty and codebase complexity.

### A10
**B) /chore and /implement**
These are the template commands introduced in Lesson 3.

### A11
**True** - Great planning IS great prompting. They are one and the same.

### A12
**False** - Templates work on small to large codebases.

### A13
**False** - "Success is planned" means those who plan tend to create the future, not that planning guarantees success.

### A14
**True** - Templates handle all these problem types.

### A15
**True** - Every plan includes validation commands to verify work was completed.

### A16
**False** - Templates let you write one high-level sentence and get comprehensive plans.

### A17
**False** - Templates create leverage for both individuals AND teams.

### A18
**plan** - A scaled prompt is a plan, a specification, a PRD.

### A19
**classes** - Templates enable solving entire classes of problems.

### A20
**create** - "Those who plan the future tend to create it."

### A21
Every great template contains:
1. **Purpose** at the top
2. **Detailed instructions**
3. **Relevant files** to guide success
4. **Plan format** in markdown
5. **A parameter** that accepts your high-level description

### A22
A Higher-Order Prompt (HOP) accepts another prompt as input. When you pass a plan into the /implement command, you're using a HOP - plans getting passed into plans. This is powerful because:
- You can compose prompts together
- You maintain and improve higher-order prompts separately
- You can swap out lower-order prompts as needed
- It enables prompt composition and reusability
